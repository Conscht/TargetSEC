Speech emotion conversion

TargetSEC

Move a recorded utterance to a target arousal level without touching what was said or who said it — by diffusing a 128-dimensional style vector instead of a spectrogram.

What it does

Speech emotion conversion changes the emotion of an utterance while keeping its words and its speaker. On acted corpora this is comparatively easy; on in-the-wild podcast speech — non-parallel, noisy, unevenly recorded — existing fixed-duration systems either shift the emotion and wreck the audio, or keep the audio clean and barely shift the emotion.

TargetSEC separates the two. A HiFi-GAN decoder is conditioned on three things: HuBERT content tokens, a WavLM speaker vector, and a 128-dimensional style vector. During training the style vector comes from a mel style encoder. At inference that encoder is removed and a latent diffusion model generates the style vector instead, conditioned on speaker identity and a continuous target arousal. Diffusion happens in a 128-dimensional space, not over a spectrogram, so the generative step never has to produce audio — it only has to produce a conditioning vector the decoder already knows how to use.

The decoder is untouched at inference time. Any continuous or discrete style conditioning can be swapped in by retraining only the diffusion prior, which is a few million parameters rather than the whole synthesis stack.

Results

 Arousal error is measured on the audeering wav2vec2 dimensional recogniser against targets on [0, 1]. For reference, that recogniser scores 9.8% Labs against human labels on real audio, and ground-truth Test1 audio scores 3.45 WVMOS.

Two measures that do not share a model with the training objective: on an independent IEMOCAP-trained recogniser, the rank correlation between requested arousal and high-arousal posterior is 0.450 for TargetSEC against 0.325 for the baseline. ECAPA-TDNN speaker similarity is 0.351 ± 0.12, against 0.370 for the baseline and a random-pair floor of 0.05.

Aggregating the 118,321 matched pairs by speaker, TargetSEC has the lower arousal error for 58 of 60 Test1 speakers (paired t-test, p < 10−19). This establishes the gap on this test set for these models; it is not a claim about variance across training seeds.

Replacing regression with diffusion and adding speaker conditioning each lower the conversion error. WVMOS moves the other way, and that ordering is the point: a deterministic MLP collapses toward the corpus mean style, which vocodes cleanly and converts poorly. Higher WVMOS here indicates a safer, less committed prediction, not a better conversion.
